Project Overview

During my internship at Boeing, I identified ergonomic challenges faced by production workers on the 767 assembly line and designed a series of custom tool attachments to address awkward grip angles on existing production tools. The attachments were designed in CATIA, 3D printed, and deployed directly on the active 767 production line where they were adopted by the teams using those tools.

Problem Statement

Certain tools used in 767 assembly required workers to operate at awkward grip angles, creating ergonomic strain during repetitive production tasks. The goal was to design low-cost, manufacturable attachments that could retrofit existing tools to improve worker comfort and reduce strain without disrupting the production workflow.

My Contribution

  • Identified ergonomic pain points on the 767 production floor through direct observation and worker feedback
  • Designed 5 custom tool attachments in CATIA to correct awkward grip angles on existing production tools
  • Iterated on designs and produced prototypes using FDM 3D printing
  • Validated attachments on the line and refined based on worker feedback
  • All 5 attachments were adopted and put into active use by the production teams

Outcome

All five attachments were adopted by the Boeing 767 production teams after on-line iteration and worker feedback. The project connected operator needs, CATIA design, FDM prototyping, and adoption on an active assembly line.

Tools Used

  • CATIA — 3D CAD design
  • FDM 3D Printing — rapid prototyping and final parts
