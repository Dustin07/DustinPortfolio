Research Context

During my in-space manufacturing internship at NASA Marshall Space Flight Center, I worked on sensing and software systems for laser directed energy deposition (LDED). The work connected a physical manufacturing process with embedded computing, multi-camera sensing, and real-time AI inference.

Real-Time Monitoring

I developed a closed-loop, GPU-accelerated process-monitoring system on an NVIDIA Jetson Thor, integrating multi-camera sensing with real-time inference. I built a decoupled, multi-rate Holoscan pipeline to keep one subsystem from stalling another, and streamed camera inference and LLM analytics to a physical LED display.

Local Documentation Assistant

I also implemented a retrieval-augmented generation pipeline on a Raspberry Pi. A local language model indexed and queried batch documentation and manuals for LDED operations. This was a separate tool from the real-time camera-monitoring pipeline.

Engineering Decisions

  • Separate processing rates: Structure the sensing and analytics pipeline around decoupled subsystems rather than one blocking sequence.
  • Connect software to hardware: Bring camera inference and analytics into a physical feedback interface.
  • Ground document queries: Retrieve information from batch documentation and operating manuals through a local RAG pipeline.
Mechanical Engineering Meets Embedded Systems

This work extends my manufacturing background into process monitoring and automation: understanding the physical operation, integrating sensing, coordinating computation, and communicating useful information.

Tools & Methods

  • Holoscan and multi-rate pipeline design
  • Multi-camera sensing and real-time detection
  • Raspberry Pi, local language models, and retrieval-augmented generation
