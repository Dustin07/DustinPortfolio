Pocket carabiner structural optimization

Evaluating how selective material removal affects stress, deflection, and volume.

Inside this projectProject OverviewProblem StatementMethodologyResultsKey FindingsTools Used

Project Overview

Final project for ME424 (Finite Element Analysis) at Oregon State University. The objective was to apply FEA-driven structural optimization to a real consumer product — a D-shaped pocket carabiner used to hang small bags. Using Siemens NX for CAD modeling and Ansys for finite element analysis, three geometric variations were evaluated to determine how material removal from the carabiner's spine affects structural performance under a 360 N (80 lb) load.

Problem Statement

The standard carabiner design carries excess material in regions that experience primarily axial stress rather than bending stress. The hypothesis was that targeted material removal from the spine could reduce weight and production cost while maintaining structural integrity within the yield limits of Aluminum 7075 (503 MPa tensile yield strength).

Methodology

  • Modeled a D-shaped pocket carabiner in Siemens NX and imported into Ansys for FEA
  • Conducted mesh convergence analysis using curvature capture, converging at 0.5mm element size
  • Applied a fixed boundary condition at the top and a 360 N tensile load at the bottom across three design variations
  • Evaluated three designs: Standard, Reduced Spine (spine cross-section flattened), and Whole Tube Reduced (full tube cross-section reduced)
  • Compared maximum von Mises stress, maximum deflection, and volume across all variations
  • Developed a stress change per unit volume reduction scoring metric to objectively rank design efficiency

Material: Aluminum 7075 — E = 71.7 GPa, ν = 0.33, Tensile Yield Strength = 503 MPa

Efficiency Scoring (Stress Change / Volume Reduction):

  • Reduced Spine: 0.0838

All three variations remained below the yield strength of Aluminum 7075, in the modeled loading condition. This is a consumer bag hanger study, not a climbing or life-safety certification. The Reduced Spine design achieved the most efficient material reduction — lowering volume by 106.6 mm³ with only a 9 MPa stress increase, while the Whole Tube Reduced design produced a 65 MPa stress increase despite more aggressive mass removal.

Key Findings

  • Removing material selectively from the spine is the most efficient optimization strategy, maintaining similar stress concentrations and structural stability while reducing production cost
  • Excessive material removal across the full tube cross-section significantly increases stress at the critical bend regions, bringing the structure closer to its yield limit
  • Deflection increased modestly across all variations (max delta of 0.31 mm), confirming all designs maintain their general shape under load
  • Stress concentrations consistently located at the spine-to-hook transition bends, as predicted by bending stress theory

Tools Used

  • Siemens NX — 3D CAD modeling
  • Ansys Mechanical — FEA, mesh convergence, von Mises stress and deflection analysis
  • MatWeb — Material property reference for Aluminum 7075
