Project Overview

Group project for AAE411 (Mechanical Engineering Applications in Aerospace) at Oregon State University, completed as part of the "Twinjet Titans" team. My specific contribution was the geometric recreation of the Boeing 767-300 main wing and horizontal tail, deriving all key aerodynamic parameters from Boeing's official Airport Planning documentation and scaled engineering drawings. The team's broader analysis also included center of gravity calculations, DATCOM stability analysis, and landing gear design evaluation.

My Contribution — Geometric Recreation of Wing & Tail

Using Boeing's 767 Airplane Characteristics for Airport Planning and scaled engineering drawings, I extracted and calculated the full set of geometric and aerodynamic parameters for both the main wing and horizontal tail. Applied standard aerodynamic equations to derive chord distribution, reference and wet surface areas, taper ratios, mean aerodynamic chord, and aerodynamic center locations. Wing chord distribution: c(y) = 11m − 0.3662y Tail chord distribution: ct(y) = 6m − 0.4296y

Team Analyses

The remaining analyses were completed by other team members and included center of gravity calculations across four loading configurations, DATCOM stability analysis at Mach 0.15 and 0.6 confirming full longitudinal and lateral stability (static margin = 0.45, neutral point = 1.27), and landing gear load and tire sizing calculations validated against Boeing's actual specifications.

DATCOM Stability Analysis

Inputted Boeing 767-300 geometry into DATCOM and ran stability analysis at Mach 0.15 and Mach 0.6 across angles of attack of -2°, 2°, and 8°. MATLAB was used to visualize the aircraft geometry from the DATCOM input.
