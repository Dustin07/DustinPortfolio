Project Overview

Group project for AAE411 (Mechanical Engineering Applications in Aerospace) at Oregon State University, completed as part of the "Twinjet Titans" team. My specific contribution was the geometric recreation of the Boeing 767-300 main wing and horizontal tail, deriving all key aerodynamic parameters from Boeing's official Airport Planning documentation and scaled engineering drawings. The team's broader analysis also included center of gravity calculations, DATCOM stability analysis, and landing gear design evaluation.

My Contribution — Geometric Recreation of Wing & Tail

Using Boeing's 767 Airplane Characteristics for Airport Planning and scaled engineering drawings, I derived the main-wing and horizontal-tail chord distributions, reference planform area, taper ratios, mean aerodynamic chord, and quarter-chord reference locations.

Wing Chord Distributionc(y) = 11 m − 0.3662 y
Tail Chord Distributioncₜ(y) = 6 m − 0.4296 y

Here, y is spanwise distance from the root in meters, and chord lengths are in meters. The quarter-chord X location uses the wing-root leading edge as the geometric datum.

Team Analyses

Other team members analyzed center of gravity across four loading configurations, ran DATCOM stability cases at Mach 0.15 and 0.6, and compared landing-gear load and tire-sizing calculations with Boeing reference data.

DATCOM Stability Analysis

Inputted Boeing 767-300 geometry into DATCOM and ran stability analysis at Mach 0.15 and Mach 0.6 across angles of attack of -2°, 2°, and 8°. MATLAB was used to visualize the aircraft geometry from the DATCOM input.

Model Assumptions

The team DATCOM study substituted a MiG-17 wing airfoil profile because the 767 wing profile was unavailable. Stability derivatives therefore describe the simplified course model rather than the actual aircraft. The geometry reconstruction and the team stability model are separate contributions.
