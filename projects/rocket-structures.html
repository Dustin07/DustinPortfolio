Project Overview

Bachelor's capstone completed with Oregon State's Student Competition Rocketry Team (SCRT) for ENGR 415. The team targets a 10,000 ft apogee in the International Rocket Engineering Competition (IREC). Within the Structures Subteam, I characterized printed PA6-GF to support investigation of additive manufacturing as an alternative to carbon fiber fabrication for components such as couplers and the nosecone interface.

The Engineering Question

How does annealing duration affect the properties of PA6-GF produced with the team’s printing process? The purpose was to collect manufacturing-specific inputs before assessing printed rocket components—not to assume datasheet properties transfer directly to the finished part.

Carbon fiber fabrication required up to 12 hours of continuous hands-on labor per component, with new tooling needed for geometry changes. Printed PA6-GF offered an alternative to investigate, but first required process-specific material characterization. Reduced fabrication effort was a motivation—not a measured outcome of this specimen study.

Role

As the sole investigator for material characterization, I owned specimen design, print process development, dimensional checks, annealing, and Instron testing.

Test Approach

The comparison varied annealing duration across five conditions, including an unannealed baseline. I measured dimensions before destructive testing, then used the Instron to characterize tensile behavior.

Specimen Preparation & Measurement Details +
  • Designed Type I dogbone specimens in CAD and documented the Bambu P1S print process.
  • Used a 12-hour filament drying protocol to address moisture-related print defects.
  • Labeled specimen groups and used calipers to track dimensions before and after annealing.
  • Annealed assigned groups at 110 °C for 3, 6, 9, or 12 hours; retained the 0-hour baseline without annealing.
  • Recorded Instron tensile results to obtain the reported tensile strength and Young’s modulus.

Results

Reading the Comparison

Longer annealing did not improve reported tensile strength. The 12-hour value was 6.38 MPa (13.1%) below the unannealed baseline. Stiffness did not change monotonically, and the reported shrink factor decreased slightly across the five conditions.

These are descriptive comparisons of reported condition values—not a significance test or a universal annealing recommendation.

Condition Averages & Datasheet Context +

Across the five reported conditions, tensile strength averaged approximately 45.9 MPa and Young's modulus approximately 1629 MPa. These are averages of the condition-level values, not pooled specimen statistics. The report lists Z-direction datasheet benchmarks of 27 MPa and 1950 MPa, respectively; a comparison requires attention to printing and conditioning differences.

Problem Solving

  • Bed adhesion: Early prints delaminated during the run. Switching to an orange PA6-GF variant and applying plate adhesive allowed subsequent full runs to complete. Because both changed, this observation does not isolate a colorway effect.
  • Filament moisture: Initial prints showed poor layer adhesion and surface defects associated with insufficient drying. I implemented a standardized 12-hour drying protocol to address the observed defects.
  • Facility access: The 9 and 12-hour annealing runs required after-hours oven access. I secured approval through OSU iLabs and completed the planned test matrix.

Engineering Use

Characterize the Process Before Sizing the Part

The useful deliverable is a traceable material dataset and a repeatable preparation/testing workflow. These can inform follow-on structural studies and dimensional-compensation investigations. The study does not demonstrate a qualified replacement for carbon fiber or measured fabrication-time savings.

Next Validation Steps

  • Recover specimen-level records: The report summarizes multiple tests but does not provide the count, scatter, or individual stress–strain curves needed here to assess uncertainty.
  • Check failure modes: Separate valid gauge-section failures from irregular or multiple fracture sites before drawing stronger conclusions about annealing.
  • Confirm repeatability: Repeat the comparison with documented conditioning, print settings, and measurement procedures.
  • Validate the application: Assess relevant load cases, geometry, and service conditions before defining structural design allowables. These specimen tests are not a flight qualification.
