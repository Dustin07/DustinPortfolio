Rocket structures & additive manufacturing

Characterizing glass-filled nylon for competition rocket structures.

Inside this projectProject OverviewThe ProblemRoleMethodologyResultsProblem SolvingImpact

Project Overview

Bachelor's capstone completed with OSU's Student Competition Rocketry Team (SCRT) for ENGR 415. SCRT competes in the Intercollegiate Rocket Engineering Competition (IREC), where the challenge is precision — hitting exactly 10,000 ft apogee with a minimum 4.4 lb payload, no higher and no lower. As part of the Structures Subteam, this project focused on evaluating and validating additive manufacturing as a replacement for the team's existing carbon fiber fabrication process for structural components including couplers and the nosecone interface.

The Problem

The team's previous manufacturing approach required up to 12 hours of continuous hands-on labor per carbon fiber component, severely limiting design iteration cycles. Each revision demanded new molds, new layup tooling, and significant technician time — making rapid optimization nearly impossible given the team's busy schedules and limited resources. This project set out to determine whether PA6-GF (glass fiber reinforced nylon) printed components could meet the structural demands of a competition rocket while dramatically reducing fabrication time and enabling unattended production runs.

Role

Sole investigator on the material characterization effort within the Structures Subteam. Owned the full workflow end-to-end: specimen design, print process development, dimensional testing, annealing studies, and Instron tensile testing. Delivered a validated dataset and standardized testing framework that the team will build on in future competition years.

Methodology

  • Designed ASTM D638 Type I dogbone specimens in CAD for standardized tensile evaluation
  • Developed and documented repeatable print process on Bambu P1S: 290°C nozzle, 110°C build plate, 0.2mm layer height, Z-axis orientation
  • Implemented 12-hour filament drying protocol to eliminate moisture-driven print defects
  • Conducted caliper-based dimensional testing to quantify shrinkage factors before and after annealing
  • Annealed specimens at 110°C across five intervals (0, 3, 6, 9, and 12 hours) to evaluate post-processing effects
  • Performed Instron tensile testing to extract Ultimate Tensile Strength and Young's Modulus for each condition

Experimentally measured tensile strength exceeded datasheet values (45.91 MPa avg vs. 27 MPa spec), while Young's Modulus came in slightly below spec (1629 MPa avg vs. 1950 MPa). Shrinkage factors were minimal and consistent across all annealing conditions, supporting further evaluation of PA6-GF for this team’s rocket structures. These specimen tests are not a flight qualification. These conservative, experimentally derived values are more reliable for FEA than manufacturer datasheets alone.

Problem Solving

  • Bed adhesion failures: Early prints delaminated mid-run due to poor first-layer adhesion. Resolved by switching to an orange PA6-GF colorway with better adhesion characteristics and applying plate adhesive — achieving consistent full-run print completion.
  • Filament moisture: Initial prints showed poor layer adhesion and surface defects traced to insufficient drying. Implemented a standardized 12-hour drying protocol that eliminated the issue entirely.
  • Facility constraints: After-hours oven access was required for 9 and 12-hour annealing runs. Navigated the OSU iLabs approval process to secure access and complete the full test matrix without compromising data integrity.

Impact

Delivered a complete, validated material characterization dataset for PA6-GF that the SCRT team now holds as a foundation for structural FEA and component optimization. By establishing measured material properties — rather than relying on idealized manufacturer specs — future teams can design lighter, more optimized parts with higher confidence in their safety margins. The standardized testing framework and documentation are structured for direct continuation in future competition years, reducing ramp-up time for incoming members.
