Custom CNC Golf Putter

Project Overview

Term project for MFGE 413 at Oregon State University. The goal was to develop a complete manufacturing workflow for a CNC-machined custom golf putter head, from concept to production-ready process.

Design & Production Targets

  • Material: 6061 Aluminum Alloy
  • Machine: DMG MORI DMU 40 (5-axis CNC mill)
  • Software: Siemens NX (CAD/CAM)
  • Cycle Time: ~90 minutes per part
  • Annual Output: 1,300 parts/year (5/day × 260 working days)
  • Sale Price: $350 per part
  • Fixturing: Compact milling-machine vise + 2 tubes connected to leftover blank

Tooling

  • Flat End Mill — 3/4 in (Roughing, 15,000 RPM)
  • Bull-Nose End Mill — 3/8 in, 0.04 in corner radius (Surface Mill, 15,000 RPM)
  • Ball End Mill — 1/8 in (Surface Mill, 15,000 RPM)
  • Hole End Mill — 1/4 in (Hole Mill, 15,000 RPM)

Machining Operations

  1. Roughing Mill (Bottom) — 2 min 14 sec
  2. Area Mill (Bottom) — 1 min 5 sec
  3. Roughing Mill (Top) — 2 min 11 sec
  4. Area Mill (Top) — 5 min 54 sec
  5. Area Mill Surface (Top) — 8 min 4 sec
  6. Hole (Top) — 25 sec

Total CNC cycle time: ~20 minutes. Total part-time, including pre/post-processing: ~84 minutes.

Sustainability

  • Pros: Local job creation, customer satisfaction, recyclable aluminum, lower environmental impact vs. titanium + air freight
  • Cons: Noise pollution, CNC energy consumption, CO₂ emissions from manufacturing

Production volumes, sale price, and financial results are planning estimates from the course project, not realized sales.
